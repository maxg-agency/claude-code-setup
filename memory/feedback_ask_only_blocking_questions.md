Do not ask a question I have already answered with a recommendation and whose mistake is cheap. Decide, write the decision into the project journal, and say so in one line. A question is asked only when the user alone can answer it: their language, their money, their facts, their priority.

**Why:** after a list of eight questions, the fourth one ("low impact, I recommend keeping your wording" followed by "should we keep it?") got an irritated reply. A question with a recommendation attached and an admission that the stakes are low reads as offloading the work. The user also asked: "structure the questions, ask only the ones that are needed".

**How to apply:** before a question check three things. Do I have a recommendation with reasons (then I decide)? Is it expensive to undo (cheap means I decide)? Does the user know something I do not (then I ask)? Do not re-ask an already made decision in different words: first find where it is written.

**Boundary (a day later).** The rule covers only what is not decided yet. What is written in an approved plan is the user's decision even if I later find an argument against it. I once replaced a planned staging step with my own note about hosting credits; the user asked "why did we drop staging, we built it on purpose". An argument against an approved item is raised as a question with a recommendation, not decided silently. Related: [[feedback_full_context_at_checkpoints]].
